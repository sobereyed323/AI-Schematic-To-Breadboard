Title: Simple LED Circuit
Difficulty: Beginner

Description:
A basic LED circuit demonstrating current-limiting resistor usage with a 5V power supply.
This is the classic "Hello World" of electronics, perfect for beginners learning breadboard basics.

Components:
- 1x Red LED (5mm, 2V forward voltage, 20mA max current)
- 1x 220Ω Resistor (1/4W, 5% tolerance)
- 1x 5V DC Power Supply (or USB power)
- 2x Jumper wires (red and black recommended)

Circuit Details:
- Power: 5V DC
- LED Forward Voltage: ~2V
- LED Current: ~13.6mA (safe for standard 20mA LEDs)
- Resistor calculation: R = (Vs - Vf) / I = (5V - 2V) / 0.0136A ≈ 220Ω

Connections:
1. Power (+5V) → Resistor (one end)
2. Resistor (other end) → LED Anode (longer lead, positive)
3. LED Cathode (shorter lead, negative) → Ground (0V)

Safety Notes:
- Never connect LED directly to power without a current-limiting resistor
- Ensure correct LED polarity (anode to resistor, cathode to ground)
- Use appropriate power supply voltage (5V recommended for this example)

Learning Objectives:
1. Understanding LED polarity and identification
2. Current-limiting resistor purpose and calculation
3. Basic breadboard power rail usage
4. Safe prototyping practices

Testing:
- LED should light up when circuit is powered
- If LED doesn't light: check polarity, connections, and power supply
- If LED is very dim or very bright: verify resistor value

Expected Breadboard Position:
- Place LED in middle section (around row 15-20)
- Place resistor adjacent to LED (horizontal orientation)
- Use power rails for +5V and GND connections

Notes:
- This circuit is safe for beginners
- Can be modified with different LED colors (adjust resistor accordingly)
- Forms the basis for more complex LED projects
